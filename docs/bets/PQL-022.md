# PQL-022: twenty-day momentum baseline

Hypothesis: assets with stronger returns over the previous 20 trading intervals may
continue to outperform over short horizons. This implementation tests mechanics,
not that investment hypothesis. Problem: the current strategies have no multiasset
history or safe multi-order rebalance operation. Learning objective: reproducible
ranking, information boundaries, turnover and atomic execution.
Generation target: Momentum Strategy, validated multiasset MarketState, and atomic
paper batches. Resolution signal: obvious synthetic winners are selected, weekly
rotation follows new ranks, and any rejected leg leaves the original ledger intact.

## Data and ranking

MarketAsset contains a Symbol, a current Price and an owned vector of PriceBar
history. Construct MarketState with a decision Timestamp and vector of these assets.
Symbols must be unique. Each asset's bars must match its symbol, have strictly
increasing dates and precede the UTC decision day. All supplied rows are validated,
including rows older than the selected window. Current-day and future bars reject;
the current execution quote is separate from historical closes.

Momentum takes an explicit unique universe of at least two symbols. Other snapshot
assets may support held-position liquidation or the SPY control without entering
the ranking. Each universe member must have at least 21 closes. The last 21 session
dates must match across all members. Missing, short, misaligned or numerically
unrepresentable data throws MomentumError; the universe never silently shrinks.

The 20-period simple return is `last_close / close_20_intervals_ago - 1`, not a sum
of daily returns. Rank descending, breaking exact ties by case-sensitive symbol.
Buy the top two even if both returns are negative; no cash/positive-return filter
is implied by this baseline. `rank(market)` exposes ordered symbol/return pairs for
inspection. A fractional return of 0.10 means 10%.

## Weekly plan

On a supplied UTC Monday, propose full sales of every positive holding in symbol
order, then buys of the top two in rank order. Split projected sale-adjusted cash
into two equal total cash budgets, each including its buy commission. Sale and buy
prices use the same configured slippage as execution. Sizing uses the existing
checked fractional-share helper. Missing quotes for any held asset, infeasible
budgets or arithmetic failure aborts the whole proposal with MomentumError.

This first version deliberately liquidates and re-enters even retained winners;
it is not a turnover-minimizing target-weight implementation. It pays all resulting
commissions and slippage. The control portfolio stays held independently.

Non-Mondays produce no orders. A missed Monday is skipped, not caught up on Tuesday.
Any accepted transaction on that Monday suppresses another rebalance. This requires
a dedicated momentum paper account with atomic batch execution and no manual/mixed
strategy activity. Future portfolio history rejects. IDs form a checked contiguous
range above the highest accepted ID; unchanged snapshots give repeatable proposals.
There is no mutable last-rebalance flag or concurrent ID reservation mechanism.

## Atomic execution is required

```cpp
#include "strategy/momentum.hpp"
#include "simulation/paper_portfolio_pair.hpp"

// costs matches the PaperPortfolioPair configuration; market is the current
// multiasset snapshot with the histories described above.
pql::Momentum strategy{{pql::Symbol::create("AAPL").value(),
                         pql::Symbol::create("MSFT").value(),
                         pql::Symbol::create("NVDA").value()}, costs};
const auto orders = strategy.generateOrders(market, pair.paperState());
const auto committed = pair.executePaperBatch(orders, market);
if (!committed) {
    // No leg was committed. Report batch rejection and refresh/retry as appropriate.
}
```

Link `pql_simulation`. Use exactly the same quote snapshot and TradingCosts for
planning and immediate execution. Market orders specify quantities, not guaranteed
cash spending caps. Do not submit rebalance legs through individual executePaper
calls: partial publication would violate the Monday completion rule.

executePaperBatch copies the paper account, runs each order through FakeBroker and
discards that candidate if any leg rejects. nullopt reports rejection, without
publishing provisional fills. On success it commits once and returns all receipts.
An empty batch succeeds without trades. Allocation exceptions before commit leave
state unchanged; the prepared nonconst result has a statically verified nonthrowing
move after commit. Start-time and existing ledger guards still apply. This operation
is in-memory, sequential, and never mutates the SPY control.

## Synthetic evidence

Over 21 aligned prior-session closes, AAPL rises 50->100 (+100%), MSFT 75->100
(+33.333...%), and NVDA stays 100 (0%). AAPL and MSFT must win irrespective of input
ordering. With current quotes 99 and commission 5, each 500 cash budget buys 5 shares:
the paper account has zero cash and 990 marked value after its initial two fills.
The next weekly liquidation/reentry at unchanged quotes incurs four more 5-unit
commissions, leaving 970 marked value. Tests expose this friction explicitly.

A rotation fixture then makes NVDA the strongest asset; the next plan sells AAPL
and MSFT before buying NVDA and MSFT. A deliberately oversized last buy proves that
earlier staged sales and buys are all discarded, after which same-Monday retry works.

## Limits

History is raw-price data, not total return. Splits/dividends, historical universe
selection, holiday calendars, missing-session detection and independent freshness
validation are not implemented. Callers must supply the latest complete 21-session
window available at the decision horizon. Date checks do not establish provider
publication/revision availability. Quotes are synthetic full-fill assumptions,
with no order-book depth, liquidity or settlement model.

Single-asset MarketState construction remains compatible. Legacy symbol()/price()
access requires exactly one asset and throws on multiasset inputs; FakeBroker,
SPY buy-and-hold and DCA now look up their actual symbol. Snapshot assignment is
deleted to prevent partial replacement of validated collections.

No persistent batch transaction, scheduler, analytics metric or profitability claim
is added. Batch rejection currently reports nullopt rather than a per-leg failure
reason. Runtime and review evidence are in `.codex/reviews/PQL-022/`.
