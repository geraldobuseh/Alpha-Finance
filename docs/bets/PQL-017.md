# PQL-017: deterministic fake execution

Hypothesis: strategies can submit the same typed orders through `Broker` while a
fake implementation executes known prices and records fee-inclusive consequences.
Problem: the broker declaration has no executable implementation or result models.
Learning objective: separate execution policy from ledger accounting, and preserve
exception safety across a financial commit. Generation target: MarketState,
Execution, FakeBroker, offline tests and contract documentation.

Resolution signal: configured-price buys/sells and fees match numeric oracles;
rejected attempts preserve the entire portfolio snapshot; replay reproduces state.
Constraints: full market fills, single currency, long-only, sequential in-memory
simulation, no network, clock reads, database changes or live trading.

## Contract and use

Include `execution/fake_broker.hpp` and link `pql_execution`. Construct
`FakeBroker{portfolio, fee}` with a trusted Portfolio reference and a nonnegative
Money fee. Negative fee configuration throws `std::invalid_argument`; zero is valid.
The portfolio must outlive the broker. Calls and other portfolio mutations must
be serialized. Strategies receive detached PortfolioSnapshot values and propose
Orders; orchestration supplies the broker and the configured MarketState.

MarketState owns one Symbol, Price and Timestamp. Each execute call fills the full
order at exactly that quote's price and time. The symbol must match (case-sensitive);
time must be at least the order time and the last accepted ledger event time.
Equal timestamps preserve submission order. Different symbols require matching
quotes supplied by the caller. No provider-specific universe is hard-coded.

The fixed fee is charged once per successful fill. The broker creates a Trade,
attaches the fee to a Transaction, then atomically applies that event. Cash,
positions, fee-adjusted cost basis and history change together. It never applies
a zero-fee trade followed by a separate balance subtraction. Rejected attempts
do not reserve an order ID or charge anything. Accepted IDs cannot execute again,
including through a replacement broker bound to the same portfolio.

Execution reports portfolio/order IDs and either a scalar fill receipt (side,
quantity, price, fees, time) or a rejection reason. The complete authoritative
transaction, including symbol, lives in portfolio history. Receipts contain no
allocating strings; compile-time nothrow copy/move checks ensure result return
cannot fail after the ledger commits. Allocation failures before commit may
propagate and leave the portfolio unchanged.

## Rejection boundaries

- Zero quantity is rejected by Order::create_market; malformed symbols by
  Symbol::create. Such invalid Orders cannot reach the typed broker API.
- A syntactically valid symbol without a matching configured quote reports
  UnknownSymbol. Quotes themselves use the validated positive finite Price type.
- Buys require cash for notional plus fee; sells require sufficient shares.
- Sale fees equal to proceeds are allowed. Fees exceeding proceeds consume existing
  cash and reject if they would overdraw it; this preserves the ledger policy.
- Duplicate IDs, reversed timestamps and unrepresentable notionals reject.
- PortfolioRejected is the final accounting gate's refusal, including arithmetic
  that would silently lose a positive contribution through double rounding.

## Numeric example

With $1,000, a $100 quote and a $2 fee, BUY2 leaves $798 cash and two shares with
$101 average cost. SELL1 at $100 leaves $896 and realizes -$3 (100 - 101 - 2).
SELL1 again leaves $994, no shares, and -$6 cumulative realized P&L: three fees.
Alternatively, selling one at $120 after the buy leaves $916 cash, realizes $17,
and values the remaining share at $120 for a $1,036 portfolio total.

## Limits

The supplied quote is a synthetic execution assumption, not evidence of liquidity,
freshness or information availability in a historical backtest. There are no
partial fills, slippage, spreads, settlement delays or fee schedules. Order stays
an immutable pending request; Execution communicates its outcome. No durable
execution repository or scheduler is introduced. Further broker implementations
can reuse the interface without making strategies own portfolio mutation.
